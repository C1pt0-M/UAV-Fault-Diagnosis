Abstract
﹛Robust fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) in multirotor unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) remains challenging due to limited actuator redundancy, nonlinear dynamics, and environmental disturbances. This work introduces two lightweight deep learning architectures: the Convolutional-LSTM Fault Detection Network (CLFDNet), which combines multi-scale one-dimensional convolutional neural networks (1D-CNN), long short-term memory (LSTM) units, and an adaptive attention mechanism for spatio-temporal fault feature extraction;and the AutoencoderLSTM Multi-loss Fusion Network(AELMFNet), a soft attention每enhanced LSTM autoencoder optimized via multi-loss fusion for ?ne-grained fault severity estimation. Both models are trained and evaluated on UAV-Fault Magnitude V1, a high-?delity simulation dataset containing 114,230 labeled samples with motor degradation levels ranging from 5% to 40% in the take-off, hover, navigation, and descent phases, representing the most probable and recoverable fault scenarios in quadrotor UAVs. Including coupled faults enables models to learn correlated degradation patterns and actuator interactions while maintaining controllability under standard ?ight laws. CLFDNet achieves 96.81% precision in fault severity classi?cation and 100% accuracy in motor fault localization with only 19.6K parameters, demonstrating suitability for real-time onboard applications. AELMFNet achieves the lowest reconstruction loss of 0.001 with Huber loss and an inference latency of 6 ms/step, underscoring its ef?ciency for embedded deployment. Comparative experiments against 15 baselines, including ?ve classical machine learning models, ?ve state-of-the-art fault detection methods, and ?ve attention-based deep learning variants, validate the effectiveness of the proposed architectures. These ?ndings con?rm that lightweight deep models enable accurate and ef?cient diagnosis of UAV faults with minimal sensing.

Introduction
﹛Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have emerged as critical components of modern intelligent systems, revolutionizing operations in the  civilian, industrial,  and  defense  sectors [1]. Their  capabilities in  executing  autonomous missions with high spatial accuracy and temporal responsiveness have enabled signi?cant advances in infrastructure inspection [2], smart agriculture [3], disaster response [4], environmental monitoring  [5],  and  delivery  last  mile  [6].  Compared  to traditional inspection methodologies, UAVs offer enhanced maneuverability, rapid deployment, and the ability to access remote  or  hazardous  environments  with  minimal  human supervision [7]. These advantages are further enhanced by continuous innovations in onboard perception, real-time con- trol systems, and intelligent navigation algorithms, which collectively expand the operational envelope of UAVs, par- ticularly in safety-critical and resource-constrained environ- ments.
﹛﹛Among the various subsystems that contribute to UAV performance and mission reliability, the propulsion system, particularly the propellers, plays a crucial role in ensuring ?ight  stability, generating thrust, and maintaining control authority  [8].  However,  these  components  are  frequently exposed to adverse operating conditions [9] such as mechan- ical fatigue [10], aerodynamic loads [11], particulate erosion [12], and thermal ?uctuations [13]. Over time, these stressors can lead to progressive degradation and abrupt structural fail- ures, which pose severe risks to ?ight stability and mission success [14,15]. The vulnerability is especially acute in mul- tirotor con?gurations such as quadrotors, which inherently lack actuator redundancy [16]. Many UAV mission failures are traceable to faults in the propulsion subsystem, under- scoring the need for timely and accurate Fault Detection and Diagnosis (FDD) to ensure safe and resilient UAV operation [17, 18].
﹛﹛Despite extensive research, developing robust, real-time FDD systems for UAVs remains an open challenge due to the complex interplay of mechanical, electrical, and software components, each with unique failure modes and nonlinear dynamics  [17, 19]. These challenges are compounded by external disturbances such as wind gusts, turbulence, and sensor noise,  which  can mask fault  signatures  or induce false alarms [20, 21]. The constraints of onboard processing, where real-time response and low power consumption are imperative, exacerbate thesedif?cultiesand limit the applica- bility of computationally intensive diagnostic methods [22].
Recent advances in data-driven UAV fault diagnosis

Traditional fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) approaches are broadly categorized into model-based and data-driven methods, each offering distinct advantages and limitations.



Model-based schemes leverage analytical redundancy and observer-based estimation to detect deviations from nomi- nal behavior, as seen in the model predictive control (MPC) framework of Mohammadi et al. [23] and the adaptive fault- tolerant control (FTC) design of Mazare et al. [24]. Similarly, Zabiha et al. [25] developed a hybrid FDD framework inte- grating  vehicle  sensor  and  actuator  fault  analysis,  while Zhu et al. [26] formulated a linear parameter-varying (LPV) model-based actuator FDD scheme using interval observers for quadrotor UAVs with cable-suspended payloads. Despite their robustness, these analytical approaches rely on precise modeling ofUAV dynamics, which is often impractical due to the nonlinear, time-varying, and disturbance-sensitive nature of?ight operations[27每29]. Moreover, they struggle to detect incipient or partial faults, such as propeller degradation, that can be masked by nominal variations [30, 31].
﹛﹛In response, data-driven methods have gained prominence for capturing complex fault patterns directly from raw sen- sor data  [32]. Recent studies have increasingly employed machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) frameworks for fault diagnosis in multirotor UAVs. For instance, Cacace et al. [33] and Scognamiglio et al. [34] utilized recurrent neu- ral networks (RNNs) with long short-term memory (LSTM) units to model external wrench disturbances associated with motor faults. Kumar et  al.  [35] proposed  a  CNN每LSTM attention network to enhance detection accuracy, while Ma et al. [36] applied conventional ML classi?ers to vibration data from MEMS sensors for structural fault diagnosis. These methods demonstrate the increasing reliance on data-driven architectures for high-?delity UAV fault identi?cation but also reveal limitations related to binary fault labeling, depen- dence on auxiliary sensors, and narrow operational datasets.	Building on these developments, recent years have wit- nessed       a       decisive        shift       toward       advanced data-driven  paradigms  that  leverage  deep  spatiotemporal representations and attention mechanisms to achieve robust- ness  in  the  face  of  real-world  uncertainty.  Within  this evolution, compact hybrid networks, graph-based reason- ing, and ensemble fusion have emerged as key enablers of resilient, noise-tolerant UAV fault detection. A growing line of research explores the use of selective attention over multi- channel ?ight data to balance local feature extraction and global context aggregation. For example, Guo et al.  [37] introduced an attention-augmented architecture for multi- channel telemetry, while Meng Zhang et al. [38] combined causal convolutions and gated recurrent units (GRUs) to pre- serve temporal  causality  and robustness under noisy  and imbalanced conditions using the air lab fault and anomaly (ALFA) dataset. Collectively, these  studies exemplify the broader transition from conventional convolutional pipelines toward attention-augmented temporal learning architectures.	In parallel,  graph  neural  networks  (GNNs)  have been employed to model inter-signal dependencies among ?ight


﹛states, thereby capturing complex correlations that extend beyond conventional channel-wise representations. Junru Li et al. [39] modeled ?ight-control variables as graph struc- tures and integrated a gated graph convolutional network (GatedGCN) with a CNN-transformer backbone for ?xed- wing fault diagnosis. In contrast, Zhiwei Wang et al. [40] reformulated propeller fault detection as a node classi?ca- tion problem on KNN-based ?ight log graphs, incorporating local and global attention mechanisms to enhance sensitivity. Complementary efforts have focused on signal-centric and multimodal pipelines, emphasizing feature engineering and denoising strategies for noisy measurements. Yongtian Zou et al. [41] derived IMU-based indicators using symmetrized dot-pattern features, while Yuchen He  [42] improved low signal每to每noise ratio(SNR)vibration analysis through adap- tive dual-tree complex wavelet transforms combined with attention-weighted ensemble learning, achieving high accu- racy on UAV-FD and DJI datasets.
﹛﹛Practical  deployment  considerations  have  also  drawn increasing attention. Ghazali et al [43] developed a fuzzy- logic  decision  system  integrated  with  smartphone-based LoRa communication for cost-effective UAV health mon- itoring,  and  Sadhu  et  al.  [44]  demonstrated  a  hardware- accelerated CNN每LSTM model using ?eld-programmable gate array (FPGA) recon?guration, achieving approximately 40℅ inference speed-up and sub-3  ms latency, underscor- ing the role  of hardware-aware  optimization in real-time fault diagnosis. Further, Yizong Zhang et al. [45] proposed a heterogeneous deep multi-task learning (HDMTL) architec- ture that combines CNN and LSTM branches with adaptive attention  sharing for  small-sample robustness, while Chu et  al.  [46]  introduced  an  improved  self-attention  LSTM with  similarity  space  mapping  (MISAL),  a  hybrid  atten- tion每LSTM  framework  guided by  maximum  information coef?cients to detect sensor bias and drift. Similarly, Huang et al.  [47] demonstrated that ensembling CNN, BiLSTM, and BiGRU networks with optimized weighting can substan- tially improve fault recognition accuracy and generalization, achieving over 99% performance across multiple fault sce- narios.
﹛﹛Recent studies have also advanced the development of graded fault severity estimation and localization. Khaneghaei et al. [48] combined CNN and LSTM modules for sever- ity estimation across 25,000 hardware-in-the-loop scenarios, and  Wang  et  al.  [49]  proposed  the  directional  informa- tion and global perception fusion (DIGPGNN) framework, which employs directed-graph learning with bidirectional aggregation and global transformers for UAV telemetry anal- ysis.  Moreover,  cross-domain  studies,  such  as  those  by Zhaoxu Wang et  al.  [50]  on  quadruped robots, reinforce the generalizability of hybrid attention-based architectures. Research gaps

﹛Despite  notable  progress  in  data-driven  UAV  fault  diag- nosis, several fundamental challenges remain insuf?ciently explored. A primary limitation lies in the continued depen- dence  of many  FDD  frameworks  on  auxiliary  vibration, current, or acoustic sensors, which increase payload, energy consumption, and system complexity. Developing a robust, plug-and-play diagnostic paradigm that operates exclusively on built-in telemetry, principally inertial measurements and motor command data, remains an open problem, particularly for lightweight aerial platforms constrained by power and computational resources. Another critical limitation is the predominance of binary fault classi?cation within DL mod- els, which often fails to capture the degree of degradation or to identify the speci?c actuator responsible for the fault. Designing a uni?ed and computationally ef?cient architec- ture capable of both graded fault-severity estimation and actuator-level localization is therefore essential to enable interpretable, predictive, and real-time fault management. A further concern relates to computational scalability and inference latency: many DL-based approaches exhibit sig- ni?cant degradation in accuracy or response speed across diverse ?ight regimes, which undermines their suitability for embedded, real-time deployment under nonstationary and noisy ?ight conditions.
﹛﹛Beyond  these  immediate   limitations,  several  broader research dimensions also remain underdeveloped. One per- sistent challenge involves cross-platform and domain gener- alization, as diagnostic models frequently experience severe performance loss when transferred across airframes, propul- sion  con?gurations,  or  environmental  settings.  Effective domain adaptation and few-shot transfer strategies are still largely absent. In addition, the majority of existing methods operate under closed-set assumptions, producing determinis- tic predictions without quantifying uncertainty. Such a lack of con?dence calibration renders UAV systems vulnerable to unseen or out-of-distribution fault conditions, emphasiz- ing  the  need  for  open-set  robust  frameworks.  Moreover, most  current  studies  regard  faults  as  isolated  or  steady- state events and rarely consider their temporal evolution. Research addressing concurrent or progressive fault propaga- tion, early-warning prediction, and detection latency remains scarce. Nevertheless, these aspects are indispensable for pre- dictive maintenance and long-term autonomous reliability in safety-critical operations.


Proposed framework and key contributions

﹛Motivated  by  the  aforementioned  challenges,  this  study develops  a  telemetry-only  fault  detection  and  diagnosis (FDD) framework that operates exclusively on onboard iner- tial and control  signals, thereby eliminating the need for auxiliary sensors. The proposed framework integrates two complementary lightweight deep learning architectures: (i) a hybrid CNN每LSTM每attention classi?er designed for mul- ticlass fault-severity grading and actuator-level localization, and (ii) an attention-enhanced LSTM autoencoder tailored for ?ne-grained severity estimation through reconstruction- based residual analysis. By exploiting spatiotemporal corre- lations inherent in multichannel UAV telemetry, the frame- work  achieves  robust  performance  under  dynamic  ?ight regimes while maintaining computational ef?ciency suitable for real-time, embedded deployment. The proposed mod- els are extensively validated on a large-scale, phase-diverse dataset that emulates realistic ?ight conditions and multi- ple degradation levels. This investigation directly addresses the  persistent  limitations  identi?ed  in  prior  research  by enabling sensor-minimal operation, uni?ed severity grading and localization, and ef?cient onboard inference. Moreover, a  high-?delity  synthetic  dataset  simulating  graded  thrust degradation  (5每40%)  under  realistic  noise  conditions  is introduced, bridging the gap between simulation-based and operational data and supporting reproducible benchmarking for future studies.

﹛Dataset overview: acquisition, structure, and constraints
This  study  presents  a  novel  multirotor  UAV  faults  with their single and double magnitude-V1 dataset, a high-?delity time-series resource speci?cally developed to support intelli- gent systems＊ advancement and rigorous evaluation for fault detection, isolation, and magnitude estimation in multirotor UAVs. The dataset comprises114,230labeled instances, each characterized by 14 carefully selected features encapsulat- ing the UAV＊s dynamic, kinematic, and actuation responses across  a  wide  range  of nominal  and  fault-induced  oper- ating conditions. Speci?cally developed for real-time and safety-critical aerospace applications, this dataset is a robust foundation for advancing research in fault-tolerant naviga- tion, autonomous fault recovery, and predictive maintenance.
﹛﹛The  data  were  generated  using  a  high-?delity  MAT- LAB每Simulink simulation environment grounded in a non-


﹛linear six-degree-of-freedom (6DOF) UAV model, incorpo- rating realistic sensor noise characteristics and environmen- tal effects to emulate real-world conditions. During key ?ight phases, including takeoff, hovering, trajectory tracking, and landing, data were collected under both healthy and faulty conditions. Within these phases, random motor faults were introduced with varying severity levels ranging from mild (5%) to severe (40%), ensuring diversity and robustness in model training and generalization. The fault classi?cation and localization performance reported in this paper are based on a comprehensive dataset that includes both single and dou- ble motor faults across all four rotors.
﹛﹛Statistical sensor noise models were applied to enhance dataset realism. Gyroscope noise was con?gured at approx- imately 0.01 degrees per second, while accelerometer noise was set at 0.0004 g. A low-pass ?lter was also employed to balance signal ?delity and noise suppression, enabling the data to retain essential dynamic properties while ?lter- ing out high-frequency noise. Each data instance includes a time index column (t/s), making the dataset particularly suitable for temporal modeling approaches such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs), temporal convolutional networks (TCNs), and attention-based architectures. The 14 features consist of angular velocity components(p, q, r), linear accel- eration components (ax, ay, az ), and Euler angles (朴 for roll, 成 for pitch, 朵 for yaw). Additionally, the dataset includes the motor outputs (m 1 , m2 , m3 , m4), representing the real-time thrust or torque values produced by each of the four rotors.
﹛﹛One of the de?ning strengths of this dataset is its detailed fault  modeling  framework,  which  includes  both   single- motor and double-motor failure  scenarios. In the case of single-motor faults, only one rotor experiences performance degradation. For instance,  an instance with motor values m 1  = 0.2, m2  = 0.0, m3  = 0.0, and m4  = 0.0 signi?es a fault condition localized to motor 1. Double-motor faults, on the other hand, involve simultaneous degradation in two rotors, thereby simulating more complex or correlated failure modes. For example, m 1  = 0.3, m2  = 0.4, m3  = 0.0, and m4  = 0.0 indicates concurrent faults in motors 1 and 2.

Constraints and scope of the dataset

﹛This  dataset  is  prepared  for  a  speci?c  quadrotor  setup, developed using a ?xed con?guration of aerodynamic char- acteristics, structural parameters, and a prede?ned control architecture. Therefore, its generalizability to other multi- rotor designs is inherently limited. Systems with different dynamics may respond differently under similar fault condi- tions, reducing the dataset＊s direct transferability to alterna- tive UAV platforms.
﹛﹛Moreover, although the simulation includes realistic sen- sor disturbances and standard maneuver sequences, it does not factor in dynamic environmental in?uences like wind gusts or atmospheric turbulence. These real-world elements play  a  signi?cant  role  in  ?ight  behavior,  especially  dur- ing outdoor missions, where distinguishing between faults and environmental disturbances becomes increasingly dif- ?cult.  For  instance,  wind  disturbances  and  partial  motor failures can similarly affect UAV attitude and stability, poten- tially confounding fault diagnosis without additional sensing or estimation mechanisms. Future iterations of the dataset would bene?t from integrating variable environmental condi- tions and turbulence models to enhance realism and broaden applicability.

Discussion

﹛This section presents a comprehensive discussion of the pro- posed fault detection framework, covering the comparative performance of baseline and proposed models, their compu- tational cost and inference ef?ciency, fault detection latency, and the broader limitations together with directions for future research.
﹛﹛The proposed framework was validated using a dataset comprising both single and double motor faults with severi-


﹛ties up to 40%, representing the most probable and recover- able fault conditions in quadrotor UAVs. Failures involving more than two motors typically result in total loss of con- trol and are therefore beyond the operational scope of the present fault detection framework. The inclusion of simul- taneous coupled motor faults enables the models to learn correlated  degradation  patterns  and  dynamic  interactions across actuators. The 40% fault limit was selected to pre- serve controllability under standard ?ight control laws, as higher  severities lead to actuator  saturation, although the algorithm itself remains capable of detecting more severe faults.  Future  work  will  aim  to  expand  the  dataset  and integrate the detection system with active fault-tolerant con- trol schemes to handle extreme scenarios. Both CLFDNet andAELMFNet demonstrate low computational complexity, with fewer than 20k parameters and inference times below 6 ms per step. These characteristics indicate strong poten- tial for real-time onboard implementation using IMU-based sensing on lightweight embedded hardware.



﹛﹛The experimental analysis further substantiates that both CLFDNet  and  AELMFNet   satisfy  real-time  operational requirements. In particular, the observed inference latencies of below 6 ms per step on standard embedded processors cor- respond to effective sampling rates exceeding 150 Hz. This performance con?rms the capability of the proposed mod- els to perform continuous, onboard fault detection without introducing computational bottlenecks. Such real-time fea- sibility is crucial for UAV safety, as timely fault identi?cation directly supports fault-tolerant control strategies and ensures mission continuity under dynamically changing ?ight condi- tions.
Comparative performance analysis
﹛The experimental results in Table 8 highlight clear perfor- mance differences across classical machine learning algo- rithms, model-based estimators, unsupervised approaches, and deep sequence architectures. Traditional classi?ers such as NV(72.53%accuracy, F1=43.0%)showed limited capac- ity for this task due to the strong independence assumption between features, which is incompatible with the coupled dynamics of UAV  states. Although  KNN  (90.97%, F1 = 83.0%) and SVM (90.19%, F1 = 82.0%) captured nonlinear relationships somewhat, their performance plateaued as they lacked temporal modeling and remained sensitive to feature scaling and noise. Ensemble-based methods such as Ran- dom Forest (93.31%, F1 = 87.0%) and XGBoost (92.06%, F1 = 84.9%) performed better by leveraging nonlinearity and feature interactions, but they still relied on static feature vec- tors rather than exploiting temporal dependencies inherent in UAV fault evolution.
﹛﹛Model-based estimators revealed further distinctions. The EKF residual每based classi?er achieved strong performance (93.5% accuracy, F1 =  88.6%) because residuals provide informative fault signatures when the system and noise mod- els are well-calibrated. In contrast, the Particle Filter (PF) baseline demonstrated limited effectiveness, achieving only 48.6%accuracy with anF1-score of19.1%. This result is con- sistent with known PF limitations: particle impoverishment, sensitivity to the accuracy of process/measurement models, and the high variance of likelihood estimates under limited observability. With only IMU and magnetometer channels, PF  struggled  to  maintain  a  discriminative  distribution  of fault hypotheses, and its computational demands further con- strained practical effectiveness.
﹛﹛Unsupervised anomaly detection approaches also exhib- ited      weak      results.      The      Autoencoder      with
thresholding achieved 54.6% accuracy and a macro-F1 of only  7.9%,  highlighting  that  reconstruction-error  thresh- olds are effective for binary anomaly detection (normal vs. abnormal) but inadequate for ?ne-grained multi-class fault categorization. This observation aligns with prior ?ndings in


the literature, where unsupervised autoencoders are shown to excel at anomaly ?agging but fail to distinguish struc- tured fault categories. Similarly, theWrench+LSTMand NN Observer baselines performed near chance level (~54每55% accuracy with F1 < 1.0%). Their limited performance stems from  mismatches  between  their  underlying  assumptions and the available data: wrench estimation requires explicit force/torque  observability,  while  observer-based  methods depend on precise dynamic modeling. These methods can- not generalize to subtle and overlapping fault classes without additional sensing modalities or accurate plant models.
﹛﹛Deep sequence models based on attention mechanisms offered better performance.  BAM,  SEAM,  and  LAM  all exceeded 92% accuracy, with LAM reaching 93.7% and F1 ＞ 88.0%. These models bene?ted from learning temporal dependencies and attending to informative time steps. How- ever, they still fell short ofthe proposed CLFDNet. This gap is partly due to their generic architectures, which lack a special- ized design for UAV-speci?c dynamics and multi-motor fault interactions. Moreover, their performance plateaued below 94%, suggesting a limited ability to disentangle overlapping fault signatures across severity levels fully.
﹛﹛By contrast, the proposed CLFDNet demonstrated clear superiority,  achieving  96.81%  accuracy  and  macro-F1  = 94.0% for fault severity classi?cation, and reaching 100% across all metrics for motor-speci?c fault detection. CLFD- Net＊s hybrid design combines convolutional branches for spatial feature extraction, LSTM for temporal dependencies, and attention for adaptive feature weighting, which proved highly effective in capturing both short- and long-term fault dynamics. This demonstrates the importance of integrating domain-inspired architectural design and sequence modeling when tackling UAV fault diagnosis.
﹛﹛These  comparative  ?ndings  suggest  that  the relatively limited effectiveness of several baselines arises from funda- mental limitations:(i)simpli?ed statistical assumptions(e.g., Na?ve Bayes), (ii) lack of temporal modeling (e.g., KNN, SVM, RF, XGB),  (iii)  sensitivity to modeling errors and observability constraints (e.g., PF, observer-based methods), and (iv) unsuitability of generic unsupervised techniques for multi-class classi?cation (e.g., AE thresholding). CLFDNet addresses  these  limitations by jointly  modeling  temporal dependencies,  nonlinear  dynamics,  and  inter-motor  fault interactions, achieving state-of-the-art UAV fault detection and diagnosis performance.

Limitations and future directions

﹛Despite the strong performance and demonstrated superior- ity of the proposed CLFDNet over baseline models, several limitations warrant further  attention. First, the evaluation relied on datasets derived from simulated UAV dynamics and sensor measurements. While simulation ensures con- trolled  and  reproducible  benchmarking,  real-world  UAV operations involve environmental uncertainties, unmodeled dynamics,  and  sensor  imperfections  that  may  challenge the generalizability of results. Extending the evaluation to diverse experimental platforms and ?ight conditions, includ- ing outdoor tests with varying disturbances, represents an important next step. Second, although CLFDNet achieved state-of-the-art  accuracy  and computational ef?ciency, its behavior under extreme fault severities or compound fault scenarios has not been fully explored. Multi-fault interac- tions, actuator cross-coupling, and structural degradation can yield fault signatures that differ signi?cantly from those of single-motor or isolated degradations. Future work should investigate transferability to compound and progressive fault modes by  augmenting  training  with  synthetic  or  hybrid- fault data. Third, the framework assumes full availability of six standardized sensor channels (p, q, r , mx, my, mz ). In  practice,  UAVs  may  operate  with  partial  or  degraded sensing due to cost, weight, or mission constraints. Devel- oping robust sensor-fusion strategies or architectures in the presence of missing or unreliable inputs remains an open challenge. Similarly, adaptive thresholding and online recal- ibration mechanisms could improve resilience against sensor drift and cumulative noise.
﹛﹛Additionally,  although the current framework employs an  LSTM  to  model  temporal  dependencies,  transformer- based architectures have recently demonstrated strong per- formance in sequential learning tasks due to their ability to  capture  long-range  dependencies  via  self-attention.  In our  application,  the  ?ight  sequences  are  relatively  short



﹛and  dominated by  local  dynamic transitions, making the LSTM a suitable and computationally ef?cient choice for real-time onboard processing. Nevertheless, future research could explore compact transformer variants such as Time Transformer,  Informer,  or  ConvFormer  to  assess  poten- tial performance gains under similar resource constraints. Furthermore, while CLFDNet demonstrates relatively low computational cost compared with attention-based baselines, UAV platforms are inherently resource-constrained. Future research should focus on developing hardware-aware opti- mization, pruning, and quantization techniques to further reduce the memory footprint and latency. Moreover, integra- tion with fault-tolerant control strategies could close the loop between detection, diagnosis, and active recovery, advancing end-to-end resilient autonomy.
﹛﹛Taken together, future research directions include: (i) val- idating the model on real-world ?ight data, (ii) extending to compound and progressive fault modes, (iii) enhancing robustness under partial sensing and dynamic environments, (iv) exploring lightweight transformer-based architectures for improved temporal representation under similar resource constraints, and (v) optimizing for ultra-lightweight deploy- ment and integration with control systems. Addressing these aspects will broaden the applicability of CLFDNet and fur- ther advance the state of the art in UAV fault detection and fault-tolerant operation.


Conclusion
﹛This work introduced two lightweight deep learning archi- tectures, CLFDNet and AELMFNet, for fault detection and severity  estimation  in  multirotor  UAVs  using  only  stan- dard IMU sensor data. A high-?delity graded fault dataset was  developed  for  realistic  evaluation,  covering  various degradation levels and ?ight conditions. CLFDNet, com- bining  multi-scale  CNN,  LSTM,  and  adaptive  attention, achieved  96.81%  accuracy  in  fault  severity  classi?cation and  100%  accuracy  in  motor  fault  localization,  consis- tently outperforming classical ML, model-based estimators, and advanced attention-based networks. Its compact design further ensured low computational cost and real-time feasi- bility. Complementarily, AELMFNet, a soft-attention LSTM autoencoder, demonstrated strong reconstruction capability, with Huber loss achieving the lowest error, and exhibited ef?- cient inference suitable for embedded UAV platforms. These results advance UAV fault detection by demonstrating that lightweight hybrid deep models can deliver accuracy, robust- ness, and ef?ciency simultaneously.
